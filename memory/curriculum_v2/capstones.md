# Valuevade — Zero to Analyst | Capstone Project Forms

> Each capstone below is written as a **Valuevade Project Form**: a metadata header, the brief, learning outcomes, the required deliverable structure, and the core — a **sequenced task list** with tickable sub-steps and explicit acceptance criteria. A student who completes every task in a project has produced a recruiter-grade artifact, not a school exercise.

**How to read a task block**
- **Objective** — what this task is for
- **Do** — the tickable sub-steps `[ ]`
- **Produce** — the concrete output that goes into the final deliverable
- **Done when** — acceptance criteria (the bar for "complete")

# CAP-01 · Initiating Coverage: The Full Equity Research Report

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Project code** | CAP-01 |
| **Track** | Fundamental Equity Research |
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Estimated duration** | 4–6 weeks |
| **Prerequisites** | Financial Modelling · FSA · Forensic/Red-Flag Accounting · Advanced Ratio Analysis · Valuation · Annual Report Reading |
| **Tools** | Excel/Sheets · NSE/BSE filings · Screener/Tijori/vendor data · concall transcripts |
| **Output** | A publication-grade initiating-coverage report (15–25 pages) + 1-page tear sheet + the underlying model |

### Project brief
Produce a full institutional **initiating coverage report** on one NSE-listed company a buy-side PM would actually act on. The bar is not "did you build a DCF" — it is "does your thesis contain a *variant perception* the Street doesn't already hold, defended by a model with no plugs and a forensic check that proves the numbers are real."

### Learning outcomes
By the end, the student can: build a driver-based 3-statement model from filings; construct a DCF with a defensible Indian WACC; triangulate value across methods; run a forensic quality-of-earnings review; articulate a non-consensus thesis with catalysts and risks; and write to institutional standard.

### Final deliverables
1. The initiating coverage report (structure specified below)
2. The 1-page tear sheet
3. The full Excel model (3-statement + DCF + comps + scenarios)
4. The forensic earnings-quality scorecard
5. Viva defence (20 min)

### Required structure of the long-form report
The report **must** contain these sections, in this order. This is the blueprint of the "full long equity research report":

1. **Cover block** — rating, 12-month target price, CMP, implied upside/downside, market cap, key trading data, one-line thesis
2. **Investment summary** — the thesis in 4–6 tight paragraphs
3. **The variant perception** — where and why your view diverges from consensus, and the catalyst that closes the gap
4. **Business overview** — model teardown, revenue architecture, unit economics, the moat *with evidence*
5. **Industry & competitive landscape** — structure (Porter's), TAM/SAM/SOM, regulatory regime, value-chain position
6. **Financial analysis** — 5-year historical performance, segment trends, margin bridge, capital allocation, returns (ROE/ROIC vs cost of capital)
7. **Earnings quality / forensic review** — the scorecard and its narrative
8. **Valuation** — DCF (assumptions + WACC build), relative valuation (normalised comps), and the football field
9. **Scenario analysis** — bull / base / bear with explicit driver assumptions and resulting targets
10. **Investment risks** — each paired with a mitigant and a leading indicator
11. **Catalysts & triggers** — dated calendar
12. **Management & governance** — promoter behaviour, board quality, related-party/pledge analysis, capital-allocation track record
13. **Financial summary tables** — P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, ratio sheet (3 years history + 3 years forecast)
14. **Appendices** — full model assumptions, peer comp table, channel-check notes, glossary

---

### Tasks

**Task 1.1 — Universe screen & company selection**
- **Objective:** Pick a name where a differentiated view is actually findable.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Screen NSE for companies ₹5,000–50,000 Cr mkt cap (under-covered band)
  - [ ] Filter for adequate disclosure, ≥5 years of filings, and a live debate (bull/bear tension)
  - [ ] Shortlist 3 names; pick 1 and document why
- **Produce:** A short selection rationale (½ page).
- **Done when:** The company is in the target cap band, has 5+ years of data, and you can name the consensus you intend to challenge.

**Task 1.2 — Thesis memo (the variant perception)**
- **Objective:** Commit to a call before the model can bias you.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Pull Street estimates / consensus rating where available
  - [ ] State your call (Buy/Hold/Sell) and the *one thing the market is missing*
  - [ ] List the 3 pillars supporting it and the 1 fact that would falsify it
- **Produce:** A one-page thesis memo.
- **Done when:** The memo names a *specific* divergence from consensus, not a restatement of it.

**Task 1.3 — Business model teardown**
- **Objective:** Understand how the company actually makes money.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Map revenue architecture (segments × volume × price × mix)
  - [ ] Define unit economics and the cost structure (fixed vs variable)
  - [ ] Identify the moat and *prove it* (pricing power, switching costs, cost curve, network) with evidence
- **Produce:** Section 4 of the report.
- **Done when:** The moat claim is backed by data (margins vs peers, churn, pricing history), not adjectives.

**Task 1.4 — Industry & competitive analysis**
- **Objective:** Place the company in its structural context.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Porter's five forces with India specifics
  - [ ] TAM/SAM/SOM build with sourced numbers
  - [ ] Map the regulatory regime (SEBI + sector regulator) and value-chain position
  - [ ] Benchmark the company vs 3–5 peers on growth, margins, returns
- **Produce:** Section 5.
- **Done when:** You can state the company's relative position and why it's defensible or fragile.

**Task 1.5 — Forensic / quality-of-earnings scorecard**
- **Objective:** Confirm the reported numbers are economically real before you forecast them.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Accruals analysis + 5-year CFO vs PAT conversion
  - [ ] Revenue-recognition quality, capitalised-vs-expensed choices
  - [ ] Related-party transactions, contingent liabilities, promoter pledge trajectory, auditor history
  - [ ] Compute the **Beneish M-score**
- **Produce:** A one-page green/amber/red **earnings-quality scorecard** + Section 7 narrative.
- **Done when:** Every red/amber flag is sourced to a filing page and explained.

**Task 1.6 — Historical financial spreading**
- **Objective:** Build the clean historical base for the model.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Spread 5 years of P&L, balance sheet, cash flow from filings
  - [ ] Normalise for one-offs; apply Ind AS adjustments (e.g. Ind AS 116 leases)
  - [ ] Build the historical ratio sheet (growth, margins, returns, leverage, working capital)
- **Produce:** Historical sheets in the model + Section 6.
- **Done when:** Historicals tie to audited filings to the rupee and one-offs are flagged.

**Task 1.7 — Driver-based revenue model**
- **Objective:** Forecast revenue from operating drivers, never a single growth %.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build segment-level drivers (volume, price, mix, capacity, utilisation)
  - [ ] Tie drivers to industry/competitive assumptions from Task 1.4
  - [ ] Document every assumption with its rationale
- **Produce:** The revenue build in the model.
- **Done when:** Revenue is explained by drivers a reviewer can challenge, each with a stated basis.

**Task 1.8 — Full 3-statement model**
- **Objective:** A fully integrated, internally consistent model.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build the integrated P&L → balance sheet → cash flow with working-capital and debt schedules
  - [ ] Ensure the balance sheet balances every period with **no plugs**
  - [ ] Forecast 3–5 years, quarterly and annual
- **Produce:** The core model.
- **Done when:** The three statements articulate, the BS balances every period, and circularity (if any) is controlled.

**Task 1.9 — DCF valuation & WACC**
- **Objective:** An intrinsic value with a defensible discount rate.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build FCFF, explicit horizon + terminal value via *both* Gordon growth and exit multiple
  - [ ] Construct WACC (Indian risk-free, ERP, levered/unlevered beta, cost of debt, capital weights)
  - [ ] Reconcile the two terminal-value methods
- **Produce:** Part of Section 8.
- **Done when:** Each WACC input is sourced and the terminal value isn't doing all the work unexplained.

**Task 1.10 — Relative valuation & football field**
- **Objective:** Cross-check intrinsic value against the market.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build a normalised peer-comp table (EV/EBITDA, P/E, P/B, growth-adjusted)
  - [ ] Adjust for accounting/capital-structure differences
  - [ ] Build a **football field** reconciling DCF, comps, and any SOTP
- **Produce:** Rest of Section 8.
- **Done when:** The football field reconciles methods and your target price sits inside a defensible range.

**Task 1.11 — Scenario analysis & sensitivities**
- **Objective:** Show the distribution of outcomes, not a point estimate.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build bull / base / bear with *explicit* driver assumptions (not ±10%)
  - [ ] Sensitivity tables: WACC × terminal growth; key operating driver × exit multiple
  - [ ] Assign rough probabilities and an expected value
- **Produce:** Section 9.
- **Done when:** Each scenario is driver-defined and the target reflects probability-weighted thinking.

**Task 1.12 — Risk, catalysts & governance**
- **Objective:** Frame what could go wrong and what forces the re-rating.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] List key risks, each with a mitigant and a leading indicator
  - [ ] Build a dated catalyst calendar (results, regulatory events, capital-allocation events)
  - [ ] Assess management/governance: pledge, RPT, board quality, capital-allocation record
- **Produce:** Sections 10–12.
- **Done when:** Risks are specific and monitorable; catalysts are dated.

**Task 1.13 — Primary / alternative research**
- **Objective:** Triangulate the desk research with the real world.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Run ≥2 channel checks (distributors, customers, ex-employees — scuttlebutt)
  - [ ] Pull ≥1 alt-data proxy (hiring, GST/EPFO, web traffic, footfall)
  - [ ] Reconcile findings against the model; adjust if warranted
- **Produce:** Appendix channel-check notes.
- **Done when:** At least one model assumption is confirmed or revised by primary evidence.

**Task 1.14 — Write the report**
- **Objective:** Assemble the long-form initiation to institutional standard.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Write all 14 sections in the required structure
  - [ ] Lead with the thesis and the variant perception; keep it PM-actionable
  - [ ] Insert the financial summary tables and football field charts
- **Produce:** The 15–25 page report.
- **Done when:** It reads like a real initiation: clear rating, target, differentiated view, and an analyst could trade off it.

**Task 1.15 — Tear sheet & viva**
- **Objective:** Distil and defend.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build a 1-page tear sheet (rating, target, thesis, key financials, risks)
  - [ ] Prepare for and pass a 20-minute viva where the panel attacks the thesis
- **Produce:** Tear sheet + viva.
- **Done when:** The tear sheet stands alone and you survive the panel's challenges.

### Evaluation rubric
| Criterion | Needs work | Good | Excellent |
|-----------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Thesis differentiation | Restates consensus | Mild tilt | Sharp, evidenced variant view + catalyst |
| Model integrity | Plugs, doesn't tie out | Growth-rate driven, ties out | Driver-based, no plugs, fully articulated |
| Forensic rigour | Surface ratios | Accruals + conversion | Full QoE scorecard, M-score, RPT/pledge |
| Valuation | One method | DCF + comps | Triangulated, defensible WACC, football field |
| Communication | Data dump | Clear structure | Reads like a real initiation; PM-actionable |

### Stretch goals
- Initiate on a *basket* of 3 names in one sub-sector with relative rankings
- Build the model to auto-refresh from the latest filing

# CAP-02 · Sector Coverage & Comparative Stock Ranking

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Project code** | CAP-02 |
| **Track** | Fundamental Equity Research (Sector) |
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Estimated duration** | 3–4 weeks |
| **Prerequisites** | CAP-01 · FSA · Advanced Ratio Analysis · Sector Analysis · Valuation |
| **Tools** | Excel/Sheets · NSE/BSE filings · vendor data · concall transcripts |
| **Output** | A sector coverage note (8–12 pages) ranking 4–6 peers, with a Top Pick and a Least Preferred call |

### Project brief
Cover a single sector the way a junior sell-side analyst does on day one: analyse the structure, benchmark 4–6 listed peers side by side, and produce a **ranked comparative note** with a clearly argued Top Pick and Least Preferred. This is deliberately *breadth over depth* — you're not building a full model on each name (CAP-01 already proved you can). You're proving you can compare companies fairly, rank them on a defensible framework, and form a relative view. That relative skill is the core of sector coverage.

### Learning outcomes
The student can: analyse a sector's structure and demand drivers; build a clean comparative financials sheet across peers; normalise for accounting differences; apply relative valuation; design a transparent scoring/ranking framework; and defend a "prefer A over B" call.

### Final deliverables
1. The sector coverage note (structure below)
2. The comparative financials & valuation sheet (the "comp sheet")
3. The ranking scorecard
4. Viva defence (15 min)

### Required structure of the note
1. **Sector snapshot** — size, growth, structure, key demand drivers, regulatory backdrop
2. **Where we are in the cycle** — the current setup and the near-term outlook
3. **The peer set** — who's in it and why, with a one-line positioning of each
4. **Comparative financials** — growth, margins, returns (ROE/ROIC), leverage, working-capital efficiency, side by side
5. **Comparative valuation** — relative multiples, normalised; cheap vs expensive *and why*
6. **The ranking** — the scoring framework and the resulting order
7. **Top Pick & Least Preferred** — the two calls, argued
8. **Key sector risks** — what would change the whole view
9. **Appendix** — the comp sheet, sources

---

### Tasks

**Task 2.1 — Sector selection & structure**
- **Objective:** Choose a sector and map how it works.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Pick a coverable Indian sector (e.g. IT services, private banks, cement, FMCG, autos, hospitals)
  - [ ] Map size, growth, demand drivers, regulatory backdrop, and the basic competitive structure
  - [ ] State where the sector sits in its cycle right now
- **Produce:** Sections 1–2 of the note.
- **Done when:** A reader understands what moves this sector and the current setup, with sourced numbers.

**Task 2.2 — Peer set & positioning**
- **Objective:** Define a fair, comparable peer set.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Select 4–6 listed peers that are genuinely comparable
  - [ ] Write a one-line positioning for each (what kind of player it is)
  - [ ] Note any that aren't strictly comparable and how you'll adjust
- **Produce:** Section 3.
- **Done when:** The peer set is defensible and each name's role in the sector is clear.

**Task 2.3 — Comparative financials (the comp sheet)**
- **Objective:** Benchmark the peers on the same metrics, fairly.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Pull 3–5 years of key financials for each peer
  - [ ] Build a side-by-side sheet: revenue growth, margins, ROE/ROIC, leverage, working-capital cycle, cash conversion
  - [ ] Normalise for obvious accounting/one-off differences so the comparison is apples-to-apples
- **Produce:** The comp sheet + Section 4.
- **Done when:** Every peer is measured on the same, normalised basis and the differences are visible at a glance.

**Task 2.4 — Comparative valuation**
- **Objective:** Establish who's cheap, who's expensive, and why.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build relative multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B as relevant), normalised
  - [ ] Relate each multiple to the company's growth and returns (is the premium/discount deserved?)
  - [ ] Flag any genuine mispricing
- **Produce:** Section 5.
- **Done when:** Valuation gaps are explained by fundamentals, not just listed.

**Task 2.5 — Ranking framework & scorecard**
- **Objective:** Turn the analysis into a transparent ranking.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Design a scoring framework (e.g. growth, profitability/returns, balance-sheet strength, valuation, with stated weights)
  - [ ] Score each peer and produce the ranked order
  - [ ] Sense-check: does the ranking match the intuition from Tasks 2.3–2.4?
- **Produce:** The ranking scorecard + Section 6.
- **Done when:** The framework is explicit, the weights are justified, and the ranking is reproducible.

**Task 2.6 — Top Pick, Least Preferred & write-up**
- **Objective:** Form and defend the relative calls, then assemble the note.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Argue the Top Pick and the Least Preferred from the ranking
  - [ ] Write the sector risks that could change the whole view
  - [ ] Assemble the 8–12 page note in the required structure and defend it in a 15-min viva
- **Produce:** Sections 7–8 + the full note + viva.
- **Done when:** The two calls follow logically from the framework and survive the panel's "why not the other one?" challenge.

### Evaluation rubric
| Criterion | Needs work | Good | Excellent |
|-----------|-----------|------|-----------|
| Sector understanding | Generic overview | Solid structure & drivers | Sharp read on structure *and* the current cycle |
| Comparability | Inconsistent metrics | Same metrics | Normalised, genuinely apples-to-apples |
| Valuation reasoning | Lists multiples | Links to fundamentals | Explains every premium/discount, flags mispricing |
| Ranking framework | Opaque/arbitrary | Transparent | Transparent, weighted, reproducible, sense-checked |
| The calls | Unsupported | Reasoned | Clearly follow the framework; survive challenge |

### Stretch goals
- Add a light DCF on just the Top Pick to support the relative call
- Track the ranking's relative performance over the course duration

# CAP-03 · AI Equity Research Copilot

| Field | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| **Project code** | CAP-03 |
| **Track** | AI & Finance |
| **Difficulty** | Advanced |
| **Estimated duration** | 4–5 weeks |
| **Prerequisites** | CAP-01 · AI & Prompt Engineering · FSA · Annual Report Reading |
| **Tools** | An LLM API · a simple front-end (or notebook) · concall transcripts + results filings |
| **Output** | A working AI tool that turns a concall + results into a structured, cited analyst note + a short build write-up |

### Project brief
Build a focused, well-engineered **AI research copilot**: feed it a company's latest concall transcript and results filing, and it produces a structured analyst note — key takeaways, guidance changes, management tone, red flags, and valuation context — every claim **cited** back to the source. This is the AI capstone, but deliberately scoped: a single-company analyst assistant done *well*, not a multi-month backtested quant platform. The skill on display is real LLM engineering (structured outputs, citations, guardrails) fused with knowing *what actually matters* in a concall — exactly the AI-plus-finance hybrid recruiters want, and it doubles as a filmable product demo.

### Learning outcomes
The student can: design a multi-stage LLM pipeline with structured (JSON-schema) outputs; enforce citations and guard against hallucination; encode finance domain judgement into prompts (what's a red flag, what's a guidance change that matters); and evaluate an AI tool's output against a human analyst's read.

### Final deliverables
1. The working tool (UI or notebook) that produces the note
2. Sample outputs for ≥3 companies
3. A short evaluation comparing the tool's output to a human read
4. A build write-up (architecture + prompt design + limitations)
5. Demo + viva (15 min)

### What the note must contain (the tool's output spec)
For any company fed in, the tool outputs:
1. **Executive summary** — 5–7 bullet takeaways
2. **Guidance & outlook deltas** — what management said now vs last quarter, and the direction of change
3. **Management tone read** — confidence vs hedging, and notable shifts in language
4. **Red-flag panel** — accounting/governance/business flags surfaced from the filing
5. **Numbers snapshot** — the key reported figures with simple YoY/QoQ context
6. **Valuation context** — where the stock trades on basic multiples vs its own history (qualitative is fine)
7. **Citations** — every non-trivial claim linked to a transcript line / filing page

---

### Tasks

**Task 3.1 — Scope & output spec**
- **Objective:** Lock what the tool does before building it.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Define the inputs (concall transcript + results filing) and the 7-part output spec above
  - [ ] Define the JSON schema each section must conform to
  - [ ] Pick 3 test companies with available transcripts + filings
- **Produce:** A one-page spec + the JSON schema.
- **Done when:** The output contract and schema are fully specified before any prompting.

**Task 3.2 — Ingestion**
- **Objective:** Get the source documents into the pipeline cleanly.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build ingestion for the transcript and the results filing (chunk if needed)
  - [ ] Preserve location info (line/section/page) so citations can point back
- **Produce:** The ingestion step.
- **Done when:** Source text is loaded and each chunk retains a referenceable location.

**Task 3.3 — Structured extraction prompts**
- **Objective:** Produce the takeaways, guidance deltas, tone, and red flags as structured output.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Write versioned prompts for each output section, returning schema-valid JSON
  - [ ] Encode finance judgement: what counts as a guidance change, a tone shift, a red flag
  - [ ] Require a citation field on every extracted claim
- **Produce:** The extraction prompts + JSON outputs.
- **Done when:** Each section returns valid JSON with citations, and the judgement is encoded in the prompt, not left to chance.

**Task 3.4 — Numbers & valuation context layer**
- **Objective:** Ground the qualitative read in the actual figures.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Extract/insert the key reported numbers with YoY/QoQ context
  - [ ] Add a simple valuation-context section (multiples vs the stock's own history)
  - [ ] Cross-check that the narrative matches the numbers (flag if it doesn't)
- **Produce:** The numbers + valuation sections of the note.
- **Done when:** The note ties the management story to the reported figures and flags any mismatch.

**Task 3.5 — Guardrails & hallucination control**
- **Objective:** Make the tool trustworthy.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Enforce "cite or omit" — no claim without a source reference
  - [ ] Add a check that flags/strips any claim that can't be grounded in the input
  - [ ] Handle missing data gracefully (say "not disclosed" rather than inventing)
- **Produce:** The guardrail layer.
- **Done when:** The tool does not fabricate; ungrounded claims are caught.

**Task 3.6 — Evaluation vs a human read**
- **Objective:** Show the tool is actually good.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] For the 3 test companies, do your own quick human read of the concall
  - [ ] Compare the tool's takeaways/red flags to yours; note hits, misses, false positives
  - [ ] Summarise where it's reliable and where it isn't
- **Produce:** A short evaluation write-up.
- **Done when:** The tool's accuracy is assessed honestly against a human baseline.

**Task 3.7 — UI, write-up & demo**
- **Objective:** Package and defend.
- **Do:**
  - [ ] Build a simple UI (or clean notebook) that runs end-to-end and renders the note
  - [ ] Write the build write-up: architecture, prompt design, and honest limitations
  - [ ] Demo it live and defend the design choices in a 15-min viva
- **Produce:** The tool + write-up + demo.
- **Done when:** A stranger can run it on a new company and get a useful, cited note, and you can explain every design decision.

### Evaluation rubric
| Criterion | Needs work | Good | Excellent |
|-----------|-----------|------|-----------|
| LLM engineering | Single freeform prompt | Multi-stage, some structure | Multi-stage, schema-valid, versioned prompts |
| Citations & guardrails | Uncited, hallucinates | Mostly cited | "Cite or omit" enforced; fabrication caught |
| Finance judgement | Generic summary | Surfaces real takeaways | Encodes what *matters* (guidance, tone, red flags) |
| Numbers grounding | Qualitative only | Some figures | Narrative tied to numbers, mismatches flagged |
| Evaluation honesty | None | Informal check | Measured vs a human read, limitations stated |
| Usability | Brittle | Runs on test cases | Runs cleanly on a new company end-to-end |

### Stretch goals
- Add a second "critic" pass: a separate prompt that challenges the note's conclusions
- Support batch mode (a folder of transcripts → a folder of notes)
- Add a simple sentiment-trend view across a company's last few concalls

## Suite-level standards (apply to all three)
- **Sourcing:** every claim cited to filing page / transcript timestamp / data vendor.
- **Disclosure:** all model assumptions stated and defensible; no plugs hiding a broken model.
- **Ethics:** CFA Institute standards on research independence and disclosure.
- **Defence:** every capstone ends in a viva where the panel tries to break the thesis.
- **Portfolio output:** a single-stock initiation report + a sector coverage note + a working AI research tool — a combination that is rare from an Indian finance undergraduate and immediately legible to a recruiter.
